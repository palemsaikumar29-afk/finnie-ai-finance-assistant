# Why Market Timing Fails

Market timing requires two correct calls - when to sell and when to buy back - and missing just the 10 best days in a decade can cut total returns roughly in half, because the best days cluster near the worst. Dalbar's investor-behavior studies consistently show the average equity investor earns far less than the funds they own, largely from timing attempts. Professionals with full-time research teams fail at it persistently. The winning alternative is boring: set an allocation, automate contributions, rebalance periodically, and ignore forecasts.
